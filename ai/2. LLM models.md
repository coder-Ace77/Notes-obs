### Types of models

#### Classification by the architecture

| **Type**            | **How it works**                                      | **Best Use Case**                                      | **Examples**        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Encoder-Only**    | Reads the whole text at once to "understand" context. | Sentiment analysis, classification, search.            | BERT, RoBERTa       |
| **Decoder-Only**    | Predicts the next word one by one.                    | **Chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemma 3)**, storytelling, coding. | GPT-4, Llama, Gemma |
| **Encoder-Decoder** | Transforms one sequence into another.                 | Translation (English to French), summarization.        | T5, BART            |

#### Classfication by what it can process

**Unimodal:** Can only handle one type of data (usually just text). The Gemma 3 1B model is unimodal.

**Multimodal (VLM):** Can "see" and "read" simultaneously. These are often called **Vision Language Models**.

- **Gemma 3 (4B and up)** is multimodal. It uses a "Vision Encoder" (to see pixels) and a "Language Decoder" (to talk) fused together.

A newer category (like **DeepSeek-R1** or **OpenAI o1**) uses "Chain of Thought." Instead of answering instantly, they "think" through a problem in steps before they output the final text. They are effectively standard LLMs that have been trained specifically to verify their own logic.

### Basic overview of llms

A **Base Model** is the raw version of an AI. It is trained on a massive chunk of the internet (Common Crawl, Wikipedia, books) with one single goal: **predict the next word.** **How it thinks:** It doesn't know it's an AI. It doesn't know how to "answer" a question. It only knows how to "complete" a pattern. Basic models are used to generate the higher models and to train them to perform some high skilled work. 

The problem with such models is that it is not easy task to force such models to answer in a particular way for example we may ask base model what is capital of `India` and instead of answering it might complete the pattern by asking what is the capital of `USA`. 

A base model like this goes into two more extra layers of training -

- **SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** Humans provide thousands of examples of "Question → Answer" pairs. This teaches the model the _format_ of a conversation.
- 1. **RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback):** Humans rank the AI's answers from "best" to "worst." This teaches the model the _vibe_—to be helpful, polite, and safe.

Now with the evolution of chat invariants we were able to provide the instructions in the chat itself and get the better response. 

#### Resoning models

While standard models focus on predicting the next most likely token in a single pass, reasoning models are designed to "think" before they speak, using an internal, verifiable process to solve complex problems. 

Standard chat completion models (like GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet) use a **Pure Autoregressive Transformer** architecture. When you send a prompt, the model calculates the probability of the next token and outputs it immediately

Reasoning models add a **System 2** (slow, deliberate) processing layer over the standard **System 1** (fast, intuitive) transformer.

The defining architectural difference is the **Hidden Reasoning Trace**. Instead of mapping the input directly to the answer, the model is trained to generate a long sequence of intermediate steps—the "Chain of Thought" (CoT)—before producing the final output.

```
Input→[Self−Correction→Step−by−StepLogic→Verification]→FinalOutput
```
Reasoning models differ in training from chat invairants. Reasoning models are built primarily through large-scale **Reinforcement Learning**.

- Instead of question+answer like given in chat models, it is given a math problem or coding bug. 
- The model tries thousands of different reasoning paths.
- Paths that lead to the correct answer (ground truth) are "rewarded" and reinforced. Paths that lead to errors are penalized.
- Over time, the model "learns" to self-correct, backtrack, and verify its own logic because those behaviors maximize the reward.

In standard models, a larger model is generally "smarter." In reasoning models, a smaller model can outperform a larger one if you give it **more time to think**.

This is known as **Inference-Time Compute Scaling**. The architecture allows the model to use a "Search" algorithm (like Monte Carlo Tree Search) to explore different logical branches. The more compute power you dedicate to the reasoning phase at the moment the user asks a question, the higher the accuracy of the result.

Finally now modern llm models combine resoning and completions by deciding how much reasoning it should and then runs the models. These are modern models like thinking model of gemini3 of google or chatgpt 5. These are called hybrid models.

### Vocab

**Tokenization:** Breaking text into smaller chunks called **tokens** (can be words, characters, or sub-words).

**Embedding:** A high-dimensional vector (a list of numbers) that represents a token's meaning. Words with similar meanings have embeddings that are "closer" together in mathematical space.

**Context Window:** The maximum number of tokens a model can "look at" or remember at one time (e.g., 8k, 32k, or even 1M tokens).

**Parameters:** The "knobs" the model adjusts during training. When people say "GPT-3 has 175 billion parameters," they are referring to the scale of these internal variables.

**Pre-training:** The first stage where a model learns from a massive, unlabelled dataset (like the whole internet) to understand language patterns.

**Fine-tuning:** Taking a pre-trained model and training it further on a smaller, specialized dataset (e.g., medical records or legal documents) to make it an expert in that area.

**RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback):** A method where humans rank the model's answers, teaching it to be more helpful, honest, and harmless.

Generating embeddings is the bridge between human language and the "math" that a Transformer understands. Since computers can't process words, we must map them into a multi-dimensional space where meaning is defined by distance. These vectors are generated by training process. 

In modern transformers the first step is input the token from which by mapping an embedding is generated. This embedding is better to use in transformer than a random integer reason being a word has multiple dimentions semantics meaning(male or female) etc. 

Note that generating embeddings is an hard process and therefore many times ppl use the embeddings generated by some other company.
